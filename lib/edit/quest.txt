I:The air grew warm, and warmer still, until it carried the breath of a great forge hidden somewhere in the deep rock; and a voice rang out clear and strong, like a hammer falling true upon an anvil. There stood Aulë the Smith, whom many name simply the Maker: mightiest in craft of all the Powers, and lord of all the substances of which Arda is made.
I:
I:It was Aulë who wrought the deeps of the earth, and the mountains, and the vessels that bear the Sun and Moon; and so great is his love of making that he is kin in spirit to all who shape and build and delve. Yet he holds no work above the worth of the labour that made it. He regarded the wanderer with a deep and knowing gaze, as a master weighs an unfinished thing and sees what it might yet become.
I:
I:"I have seen your works," he said, "the stone you have hewn and the metal you have shaped in your long going-down, and I tell you there is a true spark of the fire of making in you. I do not give my regard lightly, nor my teaching."
I:
I:[newpage]
I:
I:"But a spark is not yet a flame, and a willing hand is not yet a skilled one. Craft must be honed by trial, and the stubborn substance of the earth does not yield up its secrets to any who have not earned them. It must be wooed, and understood, and at the last persuaded, never merely forced."
I:
I:[highlight]
I:He laid his hand upon the forge before them, whose fires burned with the heat of the heart of the world. "Here, then, is my forge, and here is your trial. Take up the hammer, and from the base matter of this place fashion for me a thing of true worth and purpose: not a trinket, but a work in which your craft and your will are made one."
W:The wanderer laid the finished work before the Smith, and Aulë took it up in his great hands; and for a long while he said nothing. He turned it over and over, feeling the weight of it and the balance, hearkening as though to some voice within the metal, and missing no least detail of its making.
W:
W:"It is well-wrought," he said at last, and there was gladness in his voice. "You have not forced the shape upon the substance, but listened to it, and learned its nature, and so persuaded it to your will. That, and not mere cunning of the hands, is the very heart of true craft."
W:
W:"There are many who can make a thing that is fair to look upon, but few indeed who understand what they make. You are of the few, and you are worthy of my blessing."
W:
W:"Go now, and make, and mend," said the Maker. "May your arm never tire at the anvil, and may the hidden flaws of rock and steel reveal themselves freely to your eye, so that nothing false or weak shall ever come from your hand."

I:A chill came into the chamber, and the air grew very still, as though the place itself held its breath; and upon a throne of black and unadorned stone there sat a tall figure, veiled and grave. It was Mandos, the Doomsman of the Valar, keeper of the Houses of the Dead, who forgets nothing that has been and speaks only the counsels of Ilúvatar.
I:
I:He pronounces the dooms and judgments of the Powers, and none are uttered but at the bidding of the One; and his voice, when it came, was low and slow and heavy, like the turning of the long ages of the world. The wanderer felt the weight of it settle upon the heart, as the weight of a sorrow long remembered.
I:
I:"Child of Middle-earth," he said, "here in this dark there lingers a spirit that should long ago have passed beyond the circles of the world, to await my judgment as is the doom of Men. The hand of Túrin Turambar brought a just death upon Brodda the Easterling in the days of old, even as that man had earned."
I:
I:[newpage]
I:
I:"Yet the malice of Morgoth does not end where death begins. His black art has bound this spirit fast, far from my halls, and lent the broken thing a hollow mockery of life, so that it may neither live nor truly die, but suffer on without end in the dark."
I:
I:[highlight]
I:"This is a wound in the order of things, and it must be healed. Seek out that bound and tormented spirit, and unmake the false body that holds it, and grant it the release that even the wicked are owed at the last, that it may come at length before me to receive what is just."
I:
I:[highlight]
I:"Do this," said the Doomsman, "and you shall have restored one small thread of the pattern that the Enemy has tangled; and the way that leads deeper shall be opened before you."
W:As the false body fell and was unmade, a long sigh went out through the darkness, sorrowful and yet laden with release, and the bound spirit was gone at last upon its appointed road. A deep stillness settled over the place, such as comes only after long pain has ended.
W:
W:"The mockery is unmade," said Mandos, and his grave voice held no triumph, but only the calm of a thing set right. "The spirit of Brodda is unbound, and goes now to the judgment that is its due. You have not wrought vengeance, for vengeance was never yours to take."
W:
W:"Rather you have restored a thread of the natural order, long tangled by the Enemy's malice; and such mending, quiet and unsung, is a deed of more worth than many a louder victory."
W:
W:"Receive now this gift: a spirit shielded against the great fears that unman the hearts of mortals and turn their courage to water. Go forward into the dark, and walk unafraid."

I:A soft light gathered slowly in the gloom, grey and sorrowful, neither warm nor cold; and within it stood a figure cloaked and hooded in grey, her face turned a little aside, her eyes holding the memory of every grief that has ever been. It was Nienna, sister of the Lord of the Dead, who dwells alone and looks ever outward, toward the Walls of the World.
I:
I:She does not weep for herself, but for the hurts of all Arda, and for the marring that the Enemy has wrought upon it; and those who hearken to her learn pity, and endurance, and the hope that lies hidden in the heart of sorrow. Her voice, when she spoke, was as gentle as soft rain falling upon stone long parched.
I:
I:"Child," she said, "this place is filled with pain, more than your eyes can see. The creatures that haunt these halls are not all of them born of evil. Many are only lost: twisted and marred by the long darkness they have endured, and afraid, and lashing out at all that moves, because fear is the last thing left to them."
I:
I:[newpage]
I:
I:"I have wept for them, as I weep for all the sorrows of the world; and I would ask of you a thing that is hard, harder perhaps than any work of the blade. If there is yet pity in your heart, then show it here, in the one place where pity is least looked for."
I:
I:[highlight]
I:"Go down to the next deep, and seek out the stair that leads below; but raise no hand against the tormented things that dwell upon this level, and take no life as you go. Let compassion be your shield and your guide, where wrath would be the easier road."
I:
I:[highlight]
I:"Do this," said the Lady of Pity, "and as a sign of my faith in you, the hidden ways of descent shall be laid open to your sight."
W:As the wanderer's foot came down upon the first step of the descending stair, with no life taken upon the road behind, a deep and unlooked-for peace welled up out of the dark; and the gentle voice of Nienna returned, nearer now, and less sorrowful than before.
W:
W:"You have chosen pity over violence," she said, "where the choosing cost you something; and that is the only kind of choice that truly counts. You have walked through the valley of fear, and left behind you not a trail of death, but a small and stubborn glimmer of hope."
W:
W:"Such a deed is a light in the deepest darkness, and the lost things you spared shall trouble the Enemy's reckoning more than ever you will know. Mercy is not weakness, child; it is the harder strength."
W:
W:"For the compassion you have shown to the forsaken, receive this blessing of mine: may your path be wrapped in silence and go unmarked, whenever mercy, and not malice, is your guide."
